# Google’s Agentic Gemini, Arena’s $3.1B Valuation, and Cal AI’s $10M Wearable Agent

In a busy weekend of AI developments, three distinct stories highlight the rapid evolution of agentic AI: Google’s enterprise-focused agentic upgrade to Gemini, Arena AI’s massive valuation jump fueled by its new alignment leaderboard, and a teen founder’s $10M funding round for a wearable AI assistant. Together, they show how agentic AI is moving from conversational prototypes to task-oriented systems that can act on users’ behalf—while evaluation and monetization strategies evolve in parallel.

## Google Brings Agentic AI to Gemini for Businesses

At a Google Cloud event on October 8th, the company announced it’s bringing its Gemini AI into the agentic age with a unified agent that can not only answer questions but also get things done on the user’s behalf, all from a single interface. The agent can plan work, use custom skills and tools, and connect to businesses’ internal systems like Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Slack, Jira, Confluence, Git, BigQuery, Databricks, Postgres, Snowflake, and Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers. Users can track the agent’s thinking process, task delegation, and progress via a “tasks inbox” interface. Notably, the AI gets its own Workspace account, complete with email and context, allowing it to know who needs to approve items and what’s on people’s calendars. Early testers included sportswear brand On, Shopify, and PayPal, with Gemini Enterprise customers like BNP Paribas, Bradesco, Merck, and others. Google said new flexible spending options, including multi-model orchestration and real-time spend caps, will help businesses control enterprise AI costs.

## Arena AI Nearly Doubles Valuation with Alignment Leaderboard

Arena, the crowdsourced AI leaderboard originating from UC Berkeley, raised a $200 million Series B round at a $3.1 billion valuation on October 8th, nearly doubling its valuation in about 10 months. The company introduced an AI Evaluations service for model labs and enterprises, and added an alignment category to its leaderboard that ranks models based on unauthorized action, false attribution, and deceptive completion. Arena claims tens of millions of monthly visitors and says its platform helps enterprises determine which model works best for internal needs rather than relying only on standardized benchmarks. As AI labs realized their models were gaming benchmark tests, Arena’s community-driven approach offers a neutral third-party measurement of how safe and aligned AI actually is in the hands of real people.

[Read the full TechCrunch article](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/popular-ai-leaderboard-arena-nearly-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b-valuation-in-10-months/)

## Cal AI’s Teen Founder Raises $10M for Wearable AI Agent

Zach Yadegari, the 19-year-old co-founder of calorie-tracking app Cal AI, launched a new AI agent startup called Persona and already raised $10 million in October 2026. Persona is a personal AI assistant similar to Instinct or Meta’s Muse but with a hardware component: a $179 wearable band that activates via button or wrist flick. The assistant protects privacy by encrypting data in transit and at rest, and users can delete their data. The team baked in safeguards against phishing and prompt-injection attempts. Although the startup’s business model relies on ads, Yadegari says the agent will present unbiased recommendations because it doesn’t know which items are sponsored. The bands are expected to be available in December, and an app is in the works. Persona has already attracted “a few thousand beta users” via iMessage and generated “five figures in preorder revenue.”

## Takeaway

These developments underscore a shift in agentic AI from pure conversation to action: agents that can plan, use tools, connect to systems, and be evaluated for safety and alignment. As enterprises adopt agentic AI for real-world tasks, funding flows to both infrastructure (like Arena’s evaluations) and consumer-facing hardware (like Persona’s wearable band). The weekend’s news suggests the agentic era is accelerating—not just in what models can say, but in what they can do.
