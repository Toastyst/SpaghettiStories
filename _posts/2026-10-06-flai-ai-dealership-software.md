Flai’s AI-powered customer relationship management software is now handling over 50,000 customer appointments per month for car dealerships across the United States, the company announced in an exclusive interview with TechCrunch [[https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/flais-ai-dealership-software-is-booking-50000-appointments-per-month/|Flai's AI dealership software is booking 50,000 appointments per month | TechCrunch]]. The milestone comes alongside a $27 million Series A funding round led by Base10 Partners, with participation from dealer groups Friedkin Group and Findlay Automotive, Toyota Ventures, Y Combinator, and First Round Capital.

The startup’s software goes beyond basic phone call automation—it manages inbound service calls, outbound campaigns, and appointment scheduling for both sales and service departments. Flai claims its deep integration with dealership workflows has driven a 20x increase in revenue for participating dealers.

“Sure, we do answer the phone calls, but we’re following up with customers, we’re alerting the leadership whenever they need to take a look at something, maybe because the customer’s mad, or because you forgot to respond, or promised them something next week,” said CEO Ari Polakof. “We’ve grown quite a bit from just a phone solution to more of a platform.”

Flai’s software is also flexible, supporting bilingual conversations and adapting to each dealership’s specific needs. The company launched earlier this year with a luxury dealer in Puerto Rico where customers “switch between Spanish and English mid-conversation,” and has since rolled the platform out to all eight of the group’s stores.

While Flai’s success highlights the potential of vertical AI agents in traditional industries, other AI agent developments continue to emerge. In the consumer AI agent space, Meta’s Muse AI agent recently launched as a free, full-featured personal agent capable of booking groceries, canceling gym memberships, and haggling with customer service [[https://toastyst.github.io/SpaghettiStories/2026/10/05/meta-ai-agent-muse-internet-chaos/|Meta's AI Agent Muse Unleashed: Prepare for Internet Chaos]]. However, experts warn that the proliferation of autonomous agents could strain digital infrastructure and enable new forms of scalping and resource hoarding.

Beyond agentic AI, other AI advancements are shaping the landscape. OpenAI announced it will begin watermarking text generated by ChatGPT and Codex in the European Union to comply with the EU AI Act [[https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/|OpenAI will start watermarking ChatGPT's text in the EU | TechCrunch]]. The invisible watermark, called textGrain, shapes word choices to create a detectable pattern without affecting performance.

Meanwhile, AI chip startup Etched is reportedly fielding acquisition and investment offers at valuations of $40 billion or more, just months after raising $700 million at a $21 billion valuation [[https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/etched-fields-funding-offers-at-40b-valuation-sources-say/|Etched fields funding offers at $40B+ valuation, sources say | TechCrunch]]. The startup designs proprietary AI hardware systems aimed at challenging Nvidia’s dominance in AI inference processing.

In the pet tech sector, AI-powered feeders like Petlibro’s Granary 2 series are gaining traction, offering features such as AI-powered pet recognition, intake tracking, and app-controlled feeding [[https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/petlibros-new-ai-powered-feeder-is-a-game-changer-for-multi-cat-homes/|Petlibro’s new AI-powered feeder is a game changer for multi-cat homes | TechCrunch]]. These devices demonstrate how AI is being embedded into everyday household products to provide personalized care and monitoring.

As AI agents become more capable and specialized, industries from automotive to pet care are seeing tangible benefits. The key, experts suggest, lies in building AI systems that integrate deeply with existing workflows rather than offering generic, one-size-fits-all solutions.
